# LinkedIn post — founder voice

Written to be posted by the author personally, not by "the project". Pair with
the square blueprint film (`video/` → `F5-Blueprint`, 1080×1080, 30s).

A few deliberate choices, in case you want to edit it:

- **The first line has to survive truncation.** LinkedIn hides everything after
  roughly two lines behind "see more", so the hook cannot depend on a payoff
  further down.
- **The unflattering detail stays in.** Four or five abandoned apps, named, is
  the thing that makes the rest believable. Taking it out turns the post into a
  success story, and people discount those automatically.
- **No lesson list.** "Here are 3 things I learned" is the single clearest
  marker of copy that was generated rather than written.
- **The limitation is in the body, not a disclaimer at the end.** Pre-v1 and
  macOS-only sit in the second-to-last paragraph, where they read as confidence
  rather than as hedging.
- **The link is last and unpushed.** No "DM me", no "thoughts?".

## English

> I don't write code. I've spent the last few months building software anyway.
>
> When the AI coding tools got good enough, I did what I suspect a lot of people
> did — I built four or five things very fast. A habit tracker. Two internal
> tools for my own pipeline. Something that was supposed to read contracts. I
> don't open any of them now.
>
> Then I stopped starting new ones and stayed with one: a local-first
> orchestrator for AI agents. Same product, months on end. That's where it
> actually got interesting, and not for the reason I expected.
>
> Building fast showed me what AI can do. Staying with one thing showed me what
> I'm for.
>
> The moment it landed: the agent kept telling me it had handed work off to a
> sub-agent and was "generating now, one moment". It hadn't. Nothing had run at
> all. Once I saw it I couldn't stop seeing it, so I built something that makes
> it impossible for the thing to claim work it never did. No cleverness — it
> just checks whether anything was actually dispatched before it's allowed to
> say so.
>
> That isn't an engineering insight. It's the same instinct I use in my day job:
> stay with a client long enough to notice the gap between what they tell you is
> happening and what is actually happening. I'd just never pointed it at my own
> work before.
>
> The other thing I didn't expect: I couldn't judge whether a feature mattered
> until I could say plainly what the product was for and who it wasn't for.
> Fuzzy positioning, fuzzy judgement. That's true of selling too. If I'm vague
> about what we're genuinely good at, I come at the customer's problem from the
> wrong angle and call the result an insight, when it's a guess with good
> posture.
>
> It's pre-v1 and macOS-only, and I'm still learning most of this as I go. But
> I'd rather grind one thing properly than ship five I never open.
>
> Apache-2.0, if you'd like a look: github.com/mirzatghayrat/arslan

## 中文

> 我不写代码。但过去几个月我一直在做一个软件。
>
> AI 编程工具好用起来之后，我干了大概很多人都干过的事——很快做了四五个东西。一个习惯
> 打卡。两个自己用的内部小工具。还有一个本来想拿来读合同的。现在一个都不打开了。
>
> 后来我不再开新的了，只留下一个：一个本地优先的 AI agent 编排器。同一个产品，做了
> 好几个月。有意思的地方是从这儿开始的，而且不是我以为的那个原因。
>
> 做得快，让我知道 AI 能干什么。守着一个东西磨，让我知道我自己是干什么的。
>
> 有个瞬间说清了这件事。那个 agent 老跟我说它已经把活派给某个分身了、"正在生成，稍等"。
> 它没有。什么都没跑。看见一次之后就再也无法忽略，于是我做了个东西，让它没法声称自己
> 做了没做过的事。没什么巧妙的设计——就是在它开口之前先检查到底有没有真的派出去。
>
> 这不是什么工程洞察。这就是我本职工作里那个习惯：跟客户待得久一点，就能看出他嘴里
> 「正在发生的事」和实际发生的事之间的缝。只是我以前从没把这个习惯对准过自己做的东西。
>
> 另一件我没想到的：在我能干脆说清这个产品是给谁用的、不是给谁用的之前，我根本判断
> 不了一个功能重不重要。定位模糊，判断就模糊。做销售也一样。如果我对「我们到底哪儿
> 真的强」是含糊的，我看客户的问题就会切错角度，然后把结果叫做 insight——其实是个
> 姿态很好的猜测。
>
> 它还是 pre-v1，只支持 macOS，我自己大部分东西也还在边做边学。但我宁愿把一个东西
> 磨明白，也不想再交五个自己都不打开的。
>
> Apache-2.0，想看的话：github.com/mirzatghayrat/arslan
